About

I'm Daksh — a first-year Mechatronics Engineering student at Kennesaw State University with a passion for building systems that sit at the boundary of the physical and digital world. Growing up, I was the kind of person who wanted to know how everything worked, which eventually led me to robotics, embedded systems, and engineering.

Right now I'm doing research on a bio-robotic hand that can read physiological signals like heart rate, oxygen levels, and muscle activity — essentially giving a robot the ability to sense fatigue in real time. Outside of that, I spent the past year on KSU's SAE Formula team instrumenting a race car with strain gauges and DAQ systems, and before that I spent three years captaining an FRC robotics team through builds, competitions, and everything in between.

Beyond engineering, I hold a 2nd degree black belt in Taekwondo, played varsity tennis, and founded the Georgia branch of Closing the Divide — a nonprofit focused on bridging opportunity gaps. I speak English and Hindi fluently, and I'm always looking for the next problem worth solving.
